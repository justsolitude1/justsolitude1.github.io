The Packaging Worksheet: give what you already know a name

12 Oct 2026 • Maaz bin Yousaf

Why this matters

I started studying how creators like Lara Acosta write their posts, and one thing kept bugging me. The ones getting DMs from potential clients weren't saying anything new. They had the same tips and the same ideas as everybody else in the feed.

What they did differently was package them. The tip had a name, it came in numbered steps, and the post itself showed the thing working.

Before

"Start broad, then get specific."

That's writing 101. You'd nod at it and keep scrolling, and so would your next client.

After

The Funnel Framework

  1. Open wide
  2. Narrow it down
  3. Land on one point

It's the same advice in both boxes. The second one reads like a system somebody built, and that's the one people message you about.

How the worksheet helps

You fill it in with a pen, one page per step. Each page has the questions to answer and a worked example to copy from.

  1. Pick the one thing. Four questions that pull out the advice you give on autopilot, the kind that feels too obvious to post.
  2. Give it a name. Four naming patterns that cover most of the good names on LinkedIn, and a quick test to run your favourite through.
  3. Break it into numbered steps. Three steps, each starting with a verb, so simple advice reads like a system.
  4. Make the post prove itself. A line-by-line layout for a post that uses your framework on the reader while they read it. Most people skip this step, and it's the one that gets the DM.

What you walk away with

  • A name for something you already do well
  • Three numbered steps under it
  • A post draft where the reader watches it work
  • A checklist to run through before you hit post
